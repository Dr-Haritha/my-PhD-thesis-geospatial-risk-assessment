# Landslide Susceptibility Assessment of Archaeological Sites in Gujarat

## Overview

This study assesses landslide susceptibility across Gujarat, India, using Geographic Information Systems (GIS), Remote Sensing, and Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) techniques. The assessment was conducted as part of a broader multi-hazard risk evaluation of Harappan and other Chalcolithic archaeological sites in Gujarat.

The objective was to identify areas susceptible to landslides, evaluate environmental factors influencing slope instability, and determine archaeological sites exposed to landslide-related hazards.

---

## Objectives

- Identify landslide-prone areas across Gujarat.
- Evaluate the influence of topographic, geological, hydrological, climatic, and anthropogenic factors on landslide occurrence.
- Generate a landslide susceptibility map using the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and Weighted Overlay Analysis.
- Support risk-informed conservation planning for archaeological heritage sites.

---

## Study Area

The study covers the state of Gujarat, India, including archaeological sites associated with the Harappan Civilization and other Chalcolithic cultural traditions documented in the project geodatabase.

---

## Methodology

A landslide susceptibility model was developed using fifteen environmental and anthropogenic parameters. Each parameter was prepared as a thematic layer, reclassified according to its influence on landslide occurrence, weighted using the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP), and integrated through Weighted Overlay Analysis in ArcGIS Pro.

### Workflow

1. Data Collection and Pre-processing
2. Generation of Thematic Layers
3. Reclassification of Parameters
4. AHP-Based Weight Assignment
5. Weighted Overlay Analysis
6. Landslide Susceptibility Mapping
7. Archaeological Risk Assessment

A detailed explanation of the workflow is available in methodology.md.

## Key Findings

- Landslide susceptibility is primarily influenced by terrain characteristics, rainfall patterns, geological conditions, and hydrological factors.
- Areas with steep slopes, high rainfall, dense drainage networks, and unfavorable lithological conditions exhibit higher susceptibility.
- Several archaeological sites are located within moderate to high susceptibility zones, indicating potential threats from slope instability and related geomorphological processes.
- The results provide a spatial framework for prioritizing archaeological sites for monitoring, risk mitigation, and conservation planning.
