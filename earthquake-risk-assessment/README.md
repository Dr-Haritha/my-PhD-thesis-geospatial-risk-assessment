# Earthquake Risk Assessment of Archaeological Sites in Gujarat

## Overview

This study evaluates earthquake risk to archaeological sites across Gujarat, India, using a GIS-based risk assessment framework. The assessment was conducted as part of a broader multi-hazard risk evaluation of Harappan and other Chalcolithic archaeological sites documented within a geospatial database of cultural heritage resources.

Unlike susceptibility-based hazard assessments, earthquake risk was assessed using a combination of hazard, value, and vulnerability indicators. The framework integrates seismic hazard information, archaeological site characteristics, exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity to identify sites that may be at risk from future earthquake events.

The assessment also includes a detailed liquefaction susceptibility analysis for the Kachchh region, which experienced the 2001 Bhuj earthquake and remains one of the most seismically active regions of India.

---

## Objectives

- Assess earthquake risk to archaeological sites across Gujarat.
- Identify archaeological sites located near historical earthquake events and active seismic zones.
- Evaluate site exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity using an IPCC-based vulnerability framework.
- Generate a spatial earthquake risk map for archaeological sites.
- Examine liquefaction susceptibility in the Kachchh region.
- Support disaster risk reduction and conservation planning for archaeological heritage sites.

---

## Study Area

The study covers the state of Gujarat, India, including archaeological sites associated with the Harappan Civilization and other Chalcolithic cultural traditions documented in the project geodatabase.

Special emphasis was placed on the Kachchh region due to its high seismicity, active fault systems, and history of destructive earthquake events.

---

## Methodology

Earthquake risk was assessed using a GIS-based risk assessment framework based on the relationship between hazard, value, and vulnerability.

### Workflow

1. Earthquake Hazard Assessment
2. Archaeological Site Value Assessment
3. Exposure Assessment
4. Adaptive Capacity Assessment
5. Vulnerability Assessment
6. Earthquake Risk Calculation
7. Liquefaction Susceptibility Assessment (Kachchh Region)

## Key Findings

- Gujarat is located within one of the most seismically active regions of India and has experienced several major earthquakes throughout recorded history.
- A total of 508 archaeological sites were evaluated using the earthquake risk assessment framework.
- Most archaeological sites are located within Seismic Zones III, IV, and V.
- Forty-one archaeological sites were identified as high-risk sites.
- Seventy-one sites were classified as medium risk.
- Three hundred and ninety-six sites were classified as negligible risk.
- Prominent archaeological sites identified as being at higher earthquake risk include Dholavira, Kanmer, Bagasra, and Kuntasi.
- The results provide a spatial framework for prioritizing conservation, monitoring, and disaster preparedness measures for archaeological heritage sites.

### Key Observations

- Areas with shallow groundwater levels exhibit a higher potential for liquefaction.
- Soil characteristics strongly influence liquefaction susceptibility.
- Historical liquefaction locations correspond closely with areas of elevated seismic hazard.
- Peak Ground Acceleration values indicate significant earthquake-induced ground shaking potential in parts of Kachchh.
