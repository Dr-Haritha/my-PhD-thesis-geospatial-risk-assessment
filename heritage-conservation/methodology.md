# Methodology

## Overview

This study was conducted in two phases: (1) risk assessment and (2) conservation planning. The methodology integrates Geographic Information Systems (GIS), Remote Sensing, field validation, and internationally recognized cultural heritage risk assessment frameworks to evaluate hazards affecting archaeological sites and propose appropriate conservation strategies.

The approach was developed for 508 Harappan and other Chalcolithic archaeological sites in Gujarat, India.

---

# Study Workflow

The overall workflow adopted in this research consisted of six major stages:

1. Development of an archaeological geodatabase for 508 Harappan and Chalcolithic sites.
2. Delineation of site boundaries and generation of buffer zones.
3. Hazard assessment using GIS and Remote Sensing techniques.
4. Risk assessment using the ICCROM–CCI value-loss framework.
5. Field validation of calibrated risk results.
6. Development of conservation strategies based on UNESCO's Five Cs framework.

The workflow integrates spatial analysis, risk evaluation, field observations, and heritage management principles into a unified decision-support framework for archaeological site conservation.

---

# GIS Database Development

A comprehensive GIS geodatabase was developed to store archaeological site information, hazard datasets, risk assessments, and conservation recommendations.

The first step involved georeferencing archaeological sites using published coordinates and archaeological records. Site boundaries were then established using different approaches depending on site characteristics.

### Excavated Sites

Boundaries were defined using:

- Protected area boundaries
- Excavation reports
- Official heritage records

### Mounded Sites

Boundaries were determined using the extent of archaeological mounds identified from topographic and elevation datasets.

### Unexcavated Sites

For unexcavated archaeological sites, a circular boundary with a diameter of 100 metres was generated.

### Buffer Zones

To reduce positional uncertainty and improve spatial analysis, a 300-metre buffer was created around every archaeological site.

## Hazard Assessments Used in the Conservation Framework

### Soil Erosion Risk Assessment

![Soil Erosion Risk Map](Images/01-soil-erosion-risk-map.png)

Soil erosion assessment was conducted using GIS and the Revised Universal Soil Loss Equation (RUSLE) to identify areas susceptible to surface erosion and landscape degradation. The results highlight archaeological sites vulnerable to long-term loss of archaeological deposits.

### Flood Risk Assessment

![Flood Risk Map](Images/02-flood-risk-map.png)

Flood risk assessment identified archaeological sites exposed to seasonal inundation, riverine flooding, and hydrological hazards. These results provide an important basis for prioritizing conservation interventions and site monitoring.

### Landslide Susceptibility Assessment

![Landslide Susceptibility Map](Images/03-landslide-susceptibility-map.png)

Landslide susceptibility mapping was carried out using GIS-based Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) techniques. The assessment identifies archaeological sites located within areas prone to slope instability and mass movement processes.

### Earthquake Risk Assessment

![Earthquake Risk Map](Images/04-earthquake-risk-map.png)

Earthquake risk assessment evaluated the potential impacts of seismic hazards on archaeological sites by integrating hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and adaptive capacity indicators. The results help identify sites requiring enhanced preparedness and risk mitigation measures.

### Urban Encroachment Assessment

![Urban Encroachment Risk Map](Images/05-urban-encroachment-risk-map.png)

Urban expansion, infrastructure development, and land-use change represent significant threats to archaeological resources. The urban encroachment assessment identifies sites affected by anthropogenic pressures and supports sustainable heritage management strategies.

# Risk Assessment Framework

Risk assessment was based on the methodology developed by the International Centre for the Study of the Preservation and Restoration of Cultural Property (ICCROM) and the Canadian Conservation Institute (CCI).

The framework evaluates the potential loss of cultural heritage value caused by hazards and classifies impacts according to the percentage of value lost.

## Risk Calculation

The assessment follows the ICCROM–CCI approach in which risk is expressed as the potential loss of archaeological value caused by a specific hazard.

Risk was calculated by determining the percentage of each archaeological site's area that intersected high and very high hazard zones.

**Risk = Percentage of Archaeological Site Affected by Hazard**

The percentage of affected area was then converted into a value-loss score according to the ICCROM–CCI scale.

Where:

- Higher percentages indicate greater value loss.
- Lower percentages indicate limited impact.
- Risk scores range from 0.5 (trace loss) to 5 (total loss).

### Value Loss Classification

| Value Loss | Score | Priority | Interpretation |
|------------|--------|----------|---------------|
| 100–60% | 5 | High | Total or almost total loss |
| 60–20% | 4.5 | High | Severe damage |
| 20–6% | 4 | Medium | Large loss |
| 6–2% | 3.5 | Medium | Moderate loss |
| 2–0.6% | 3 | Medium | Small loss |
| 0.6–0.2% | 2.5 | Negligible | Minor loss |
| 0.2–0.06% | 2 | Negligible | Tiny loss |
| 0.06–0.02% | 1.5 | Negligible | Trace loss |
| 0.02–0.006% | 1 | Negligible | Very small trace loss |
| 0.006–0.002% | 0.5 | Negligible | Minimal loss |

# Archaeological Value Assessment

Cultural heritage value may include:

- Historical value
- Aesthetic value
- Scientific value
- Economic value
- Religious value
- Social value
- Ecological value
- Political value

However, most archaeological sites examined in this study are subsurface sites with limited standing architecture.

Consequently, many traditional value categories could not be consistently measured across all sites.

Therefore, physical value was selected as the primary assessment criterion.

Physical value refers to:

- The physical extent of archaeological deposits
- Preserved archaeological structures
- Site area and integrity

Out of the various cultural heritage values discussed in the literature, physical value was identified as the most suitable proxy for archaeological sites in Gujarat because most sites lack visible standing architecture, active religious functions, documented visitor statistics, or measurable economic indicators.

---

# Hazard Assessment

Hazard layers were generated for the entire state of Gujarat using GIS and Remote Sensing techniques.

The following hazards were evaluated:

- Floods
- Soil erosion
- Landslides
- Earthquakes
- Vegetation growth
- Urban development
- Vandalism

Individual hazard maps were developed and subsequently integrated with archaeological site boundaries and buffer zones.

The percentage of each archaeological site affected by high and very high hazard classes was calculated.

The resulting percentage was then converted into a value-loss score using the ICCROM–CCI framework.

---

# Field Validation

Fieldwork was conducted to validate GIS-based risk assessment results and provide ground verification of mapped hazards.

A total of 38 archaeological sites were visited across Gujarat.

Site selection was based on:

- Protected status
- Accessibility
- Hazard exposure
- Geographic distribution

Field validation formed a critical component of the study by providing ground-truth evidence for the remotely sensed and GIS-derived risk assessments.

---

## Field Survey Design

A structured questionnaire was developed and implemented during field investigations.

The questionnaire consisted of three sections:

### Part I: Site Information

This section recorded:

- Site name
- Village
- Taluka
- District
- Geographic coordinates
- Ownership status

Ownership categories included:

- Protected sites
- Unprotected sites
- Agricultural land
- Residential areas

### Part II: Archaeological Remains

This section documented the presence of archaeological evidence.

Observed remains included:

- Structural remains
- Pottery
- Bones
- Shells
- Stone artefacts

### Part III: Risk Verification

The final section was used to validate the hazards identified through GIS analysis.

Hazards assessed included:

- Soil erosion
- Flooding
- Vegetation growth
- Minor vandalism

Earthquake impacts could not be directly validated because most archaeological deposits are subsurface.

---

## Hazard Verification Categories

### Soil Erosion

Soil erosion was classified as:

- Rill erosion (≤ 30 cm)
- Gully erosion (> 30 cm)

### Flooding

Floods were categorized according to water depth:

- Small-scale flooding (< 1 m)
- Large-scale flooding (> 1 m)

### Vegetation

Vegetation growth was classified as:

- Weeds
- Dense vegetation

### Vandalism

Vandalism was classified as:

- Littering
- Structural damage
- Alternative land use

Examples included:

- Grazing
- Cattle shelters
- Encroachment

# Conservation Strategy Framework

The second phase of the research focused on conservation planning.

Conservation strategies were developed for archaeological sites identified as being at high risk.

The framework was adapted from the strategic objectives adopted by the UNESCO World Heritage Committee and commonly known as the Five Cs.

The Five Cs include:

1. Credibility
2. Conservation
3. Capacity-Building
4. Communication
5. Communities

---

# Credibility

This component focuses on improving recognition and protection of archaeological sites.

The study identified archaeological sites that may warrant additional legal protection based on their significance and risk exposure.

The framework also highlights limitations within existing protection systems and encourages broader recognition of archaeological heritage.

---

# Conservation

Conservation focuses on reducing risks identified through spatial analysis.

The proposed measures are hazard-specific and include:

- Site monitoring
- Physical stabilization
- Erosion control
- Flood mitigation
- Vegetation management
- Protection from urban encroachment

Priority is given to sites classified as high risk.

---

# Capacity-Building

The study recognizes the need to strengthen heritage management practices in India.

Recommendations include:

- Interdisciplinary collaboration
- Training programmes
- Technical capacity development
- Integration of GIS and Remote Sensing into heritage management
- Improved institutional coordination

---

# Communication

Effective communication is essential for long-term heritage protection.

To improve accessibility and public awareness, risk assessment results were disseminated through digital platforms.

The study also proposes the use of digital visualization technologies to improve public engagement.

---

## Digital Documentation and 3D Visualization

![3D Visualization Workflow](Images/06-3d-visualization-workflow.png)

A framework was developed to generate digital 3D reconstructions of archaeological sites.

The workflow includes:

1. Collection of excavation reports
2. Compilation of site plans and photographs
3. Creation of 3D models in SketchUp Pro
4. Generation of Digital Terrain Models (DTMs)
5. Integration of 3D models into ArcGIS Pro
6. Development of georeferenced archaeological visualizations

The resulting models contain:

- Terrain information
- Site layouts
- Three-dimensional reconstructions

This approach provides a cost-effective alternative to photogrammetric documentation while supporting heritage communication, education, and public outreach.

---

# Communities

Community participation is recognized as a critical component of sustainable heritage management.

The study emphasizes:

- Public participation
- Community engagement
- Local knowledge integration
- Awareness programmes
- Heritage stewardship

Greater involvement of local communities can improve monitoring, protection, and long-term conservation outcomes.

---

# Decision Support Framework

The methodology was designed not only to identify hazards but also to support heritage management decisions.

The final outputs enable:

- Identification of high-risk archaeological sites
- Prioritization of conservation interventions
- Allocation of management resources
- Monitoring of vulnerable sites
- Integration of risk information into heritage planning

The framework provides a reproducible approach that can be adapted to archaeological landscapes in other regions facing similar environmental and anthropogenic threats.

---

# Final Geodatabase

The final geodatabase developed in this study contains five primary attributes:

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|------------|
| Name | Archaeological site name |
| Coordinates | Geographic location |
| Hazard | Hazard exposure information |
| Risk | Risk classification |
| Conservation Strategies | Recommended management actions |

The geodatabase serves as a decision-support tool for archaeologists, heritage managers, planners, policymakers, and conservation professionals.

It provides an integrated framework linking hazard assessment, risk evaluation, field validation, conservation planning, and heritage management for archaeological sites across Gujarat.
